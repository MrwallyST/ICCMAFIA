## 2. The Foundation: Why Higher Time Frames Matter
In this strategy, the Daily time frame is your anchor. It tells you who is actually in control of the market. To read this chart, you must identify the **Swing Low**—the specific point where buyers stepped in and pushed the price back up.

The fundamental rule for finding high-probability sells is simple: price must **bypass** or break through that Daily swing low. If the price hasn’t bypassed that level, the buyers are still there. A break signifies that the sellers have successfully overwhelmed the previous "fuel" for the move.

> **Entry for Sells = Bypassing a Daily Swing Low.**

## 3. The ICC Framework: Indication, Correction, Continuation
The "ICC" framework is the lifecycle of a professional trade. It’s the logic that ensures you aren't just chasing price, but following market structure.

*   **Indication:** This is the breakout. It occurs when price breaks under a swing low (for a sell) or over a swing high (for a buy). It is the market’s way of showing its hand.
*   **Correction:** Price rarely moves in a straight line. The correction is a temporary move against the trend (e.g., a bullish move during a bearish trend). This phase is actually a lower-time-frame uptrend that you must wait for the market to exhaust.
*   **Continuation:** This is where the money is made. The continuation begins when the correction stops making new highs/lows and aligns back with the original "Indication."

## 4. Identifying the "Creator" vs. the "Product"
To master structure, you must understand the relationship between the cause and the effect.

*   **The Creator:** This is the original high or low that *caused* a specific price movement. It is the source of the momentum.
*   **The Product:** This is the resulting price action—the new high or low created by the move.

The market is always trying to "take out" old Creators. In a bearish trend, nothing should ever be higher than the Original Creator. If price breaks above the level that created the sell-off, your thesis is invalidated, and the trend has reversed.

> **Coach’s Tip:** Don’t get distracted by the "body vs. wick" debate. Whether you mark your levels at the candle body or the wick, they both represent the same area of market interest. Either one works—consistency is what matters.

## 5. Refining the Entry: The 4-Hour and 1-Hour Synergy
While the Daily chart provides the "what," the 4H and 1H charts provide the "when." This synergy is where we find Market Alignment.

1.  **Preparation (4H):** Scale down to the 4H chart to look for the ICC pattern. You are looking for the mid-term trend to begin showing signs of weakness as it approaches your Daily level.
2.  **Execution (1H):** If the 4H is unclear, the 1H chart reveals the transition faster. When the 1H transitions from its uptrend (the correction) back to a downtrend by breaking its internal structure, you have your signal.
3.  **Alignment:** The highest probability trades occur when the 1H, 4H, and Daily are all bearish. When all three time frames agree, you have maximum volume and intent behind your move.

## 6. Execution: Timing and Risk Management
A great strategy without precise execution is useless. Use these rules to protect your capital:

*   **Session Volume:** Volume is the fuel that allows price to bypass key levels. While the London session provides great moves, **New York is highly, highly reliable.** Always aim to trade during these high-volume windows.
*   **The Power of Lower Highs:** In our case study, the market formed **six lower highs** on the 15-minute and 1-hour charts *before* the Daily level was even bypassed. This was the market screaming that buyers were exhausted and a breakdown was imminent.
*   **Stop Loss Placement:** Your stop loss must go above the **Creator** of the current move (the previous lower high). If price returns to this level, the bearish thesis is over. 
*   **Targeting:** We target "old lows"—the original Creators of previous moves. By aligning with the higher time frame, we can achieve massive returns. In the case study provided, this strategy secured a **1-to-7 risk-reward ratio.**
