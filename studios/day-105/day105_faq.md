# Day Trading Crash Course #5: The Daily & 4H Combination Strategy

### 1. Strategic Overview: The Multi-Timeframe Approach
My methodology centers on the ruthless simplification of market noise. In a world of over-complicated indicators, I choose to lean on the higher timeframes to find the truth. As a swing trader, my goal is to ignore intraday fluctuations and capitalize on the massive shifts in momentum that only become clear when you zoom out.

The **Daily and 4H Combination** is the foundation of my strategy. In this system, the Daily timeframe serves as the absolute anchor; it provides the "Indication" that dictates our bias. We then use the 4-Hour and 1-Hour charts to refine that vision. Success in this strategy requires a mastery of the **ICC Framework** (Indication, Correction, Continuation), which allows us to identify exactly when a market "fight" has been won and when it is safe to join the prevailing trend.

---

### 2. The ICC Framework Defined
The ICC framework is how we categorize every movement the market makes. We do not guess; we wait for the market to move through these three phases.

*   **Indication (Daily Level):** This is a breakout of a significant level. It occurs when price aggressively breaks past previous swing highs or lows. On the Daily, this signals the ultimate intent of the big players.
*   **Correction (4H/1H Level):** This is the counter-trend "breath" the market takes. If the Daily indication is bearish, the correction will be a temporary bullish rally. 
*   **Continuation (Execution):** This is confirmed when the correction’s internal trend is physically broken. You must see the correction lose its "creator" level to confirm the original trend is resuming.

| Phase | Bullish Scenario | Bearish Scenario |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Indication** | Price breaks above a Daily swing high. | Price breaks below a Daily swing low. |
| **Correction** | Price moves downward (bearish) on the 4H. | Price moves upward (bullish) on the 4H. |
| **Continuation** | Price breaks the correction’s "creator" high. | Price breaks the correction’s "creator" low. |

### 3. Core Technical Concepts & Market Geometry

**The "110 Rule" for Sell Entries**
I follow a strict technical standard: **The 110 Rule.** To enter a sell position, price *must* break a swing low. If it hasn't bypassed a swing low, the sellers haven't proven control. No break, no trade.

**The "Creator vs. Product" Analogy**
Think of market structure as a relationship between a **Creator** and a **Product**. 
*   The **Creator** is the specific low that produced a new high (or the high that produced a low).
*   The **Product** is the resulting move.
*   **The Rule:** Price is not supposed to break where the original creation came from. In an uptrend, nothing can be lower than the original creator. If price breaks that creator, the "product" has failed, and the trend has reversed. This is the ultimate level of respect in market geometry.

### 4. Multi-Timeframe Hierarchy and Roles
You must understand the specific job of each timeframe to keep your analysis clean:

*   **Daily Timeframe:** The source of the **Indication**. Use this to identify major swing points and the primary direction of momentum.
*   **4-Hour Timeframe:** The primary entry timeframe. This allows you to see the internal structure of the Daily moves and prepare for the breakout of Daily levels.
*   **1-Hour Timeframe:** Use this to observe trend transitions in detail. If the 4H structure is muddy, the 1H will reveal the specific "Creator" levels being broken.
*   **15-Minute Timeframe:** Used for micro-alignment. You are looking for a consistent rhythm of lower lows and lower highs to confirm that every level of the market is in sync with your Daily bias.

### 5. Trade Execution: The Sell Checklist
When executing a "Sell" trade, you must verify these steps in order:

1.  **Identify Daily Indication:** Confirm price has broken a major Daily swing low.
2.  **Monitor the Correction:** Wait for the 4H/1H bullish correction to occur.
3.  **Find the Creator:** Identify the low that created the highest point of the correction.
4.  **The 110 Break:** Wait for price to break below that "creator" low or a significant swing low.
5.  **Observe the Fight:** Look for sellers to actively defend the mid-range (creating lower highs).
6.  **Verify Session Timing:** Only execute during high-volume periods. The **London and New York sessions** provide the liquidity necessary for these moves to sustain momentum.

### 6. Case Study: Selling into the Support "Trap"
A common mistake traders make is refusing to sell when price is at a Daily support level. In a recent trade, I did the opposite.

*   **The Setup:** Price was sitting at a major Daily support level—a zone where buyers usually step in. However, the lower timeframes told a different story. 
*   **The Fight:** Instead of a sharp bounce, I observed a "fight." Sellers were holding their ground mid-range, preventing price from returning to its previous highs.
*   **The Threshold of Exhaustion:** I identified **six consecutive lower highs** on the lower timeframes. This persistent failure of buyers to push price up, even at a major support level, was my signal that the support was a trap and was about to fail.
*   **Execution & Risk:** I entered the short as price rejected the upside for the sixth time. My stop-loss was placed above the previous "creator" (the last lower high). 
*   **Result:** By targeting "old lows" (the origin of previous bullish moves), the trade realized a **1:7 reward-to-risk ratio**.
