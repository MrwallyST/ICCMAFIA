# Mastering Chart Markups for Trend Trading

Mastering chart markups is the difference between guessing and trading with institutional precision. To identify true market structure, you must master the 1-hour and 4-hour timeframes before ever looking for an entry. 

HTF analysis dictates LTF execution. Let’s break it down. 🧵

2/ Start on the 1H timeframe to define the "Master Structure." You need to identify:
• Current price location
• The absolute highest highs and lowest lows
• The closest swing highs/lows to price

These levels are the origins of momentum. Important: The level where bullish momentum originated isn't just support—it is your primary profit target when the trend eventually flips bearish.

3/ Trend confirmation is found in HTF swing levels:
• Uptrend: Sequence of Higher Highs (HH) and Higher Lows (HL).
• Downtrend: Sequence of Lower Highs (LH) and Lower Lows (LL).

These swing levels hold significantly more weight than the "noise" on a 5M or 15M chart. If the 1H hasn't shifted, the LTF "trend" is often just a trap.

4/ I use "Yellow Boxes" to mark the current swing range (the space between the current HH and HL). 

This is your "waiting zone." 
• Break above the box: Sellers are cleared; buyers are in total control.
• Break below the box: Buyers have lost the level. 
Don’t trade inside the box—wait for the breakout to confirm the next leg.

5/ Volume is your shield against liquidity grabs. When trading GU (GBP/USD), the London Open is critical. 

Watch for the "Indication and Correction" cycle:
• Indication: A heavy session push (often sets the weekly direction on Mon/Tue).
• Correction: A retracement back to a key level.
Trading inside session volume ensures you aren't hunted by news spikes or low-liquidity manipulations.

6/ Once the 1H trend is set, scale down for a precision entry. 

If the 1H is bearish, your mindset must be clinical: Ignore all bullish signals on the 5M. You are exclusively hunting for bearish structure and momentum during high-volume session windows. If the HTF is the North Star, the LTF is just the timing tool.

7/ There is an "early entry" for risk-takers, but "The Safe Way" is more consistent: 

Wait for price to return to a support/resistance level on the 5M/15M. Watch the buyers and sellers fight. The entry signal isn't just the touch—it’s the physical break of the structure that was holding price. When support breaks after a failed buy push, the path is clear.

8/ Aim for 1:4 Risk-to-Reward ratios. 

You don’t always need a hard TP on the platform. Set alerts at previous swing lows/highs (your HTF targets) and monitor the 5M structure. If price is making LLs and LHs, hold partials and trail your stop above the new 5M lower highs to maximize the run.

9/ The Professional Process:
1. Determine 1H Trend (The Filter)
2. Wait for Indication/Correction (The Setup)
3. Scale to 5M/15M during London volume (The Execution)

Golden Rule: The 1H timeframe is the ultimate filter for market noise. If you stick to HTF swing levels and trade with session volume, you’ll stop being the liquidity and start trading it. 📈
