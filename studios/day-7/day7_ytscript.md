## 2. The Foundation: Identifying Key Levels and Market Structure

The markup process begins by stripping away the noise and looking at the "Big Three" timeframes: the Daily, the 4-hour, and the 1-hour. We start here because these levels hold significantly more weight than anything you’ll see on a 5-minute chart.

### The "First Look" Protocol
*   **Locate Current Price:** Find exactly where the market is trading right now.
*   **Establish the Ceiling and Floor:** Identify the highest highs and the closest significant highs and lows relative to current price on the Daily and 1-hour charts.
*   **Confirm the Trend:** Mark your "Higher Highs" (HH) and "Higher Lows" (HL). If the market is consistently printing these, you are in a confirmed uptrend.
*   **Identify Momentum Sources:** Trace back to where the bullish momentum started. This is your "Support Level."

**The Strategic Role of Support**
Support levels are the areas where buyers showed their hand. I mark these clearly because they serve two purposes: they are the "bouncers" keeping the uptrend alive, and they are our **primary targets** if the market eventually shifts direction. If price breaks under these levels, it’s a massive signal that the buyers who were holding the market up have been liquidated or have stepped aside.

## 3. Analyzing Trend Transitions: The Narrative of the Trade

Markets don’t just flip on a dime; they provide clues. Your job is to watch the reaction at the levels we just marked. If an uptrend is going to fail, it usually follows a specific sequence: the momentum stalls, a standoff occurs, and then the "breakout" happens during a high-volume session.

### Trend Transition Signals

| Signal | Market Implication |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Failure to Break Previous High** | Buyers are exhausted; they can no longer push price to new territory. |
| **Formation of LH and LL** | A "Lower High" followed by a "Lower Low" confirms the trend has flipped to bearish. |
| **Breaking Major Support** | The "floor" has given way; sellers are now in full control of the narrative. |

### The "Yellow Box" Standoff
When price is trapped between a previous high and a previous low, I use a **Yellow Box** to mark the zone. This represents a standoff. On one side, you have sellers trying to push price down; on the other, buyers are defending the support. We don’t guess who wins. We wait for the London session volume to provide the "indication" of the winner. If price breaks below the box, the sellers have won, and we look for the sell.

## 4. The Critical Role of Trading Sessions and Volume

Structure is the map, but volume is the fuel. For pairs like **GU (GBP/USD)**, the London session is everything. This is when the real "indication" of the day’s direction happens.

### The Indication and Correction Cycle
*   **Indication:** This is the initial heavy push during London session volume that dictates the day's trend. 
*   **Correction:** Once the indication is made, price will often pull back. This correction can happen later in the same day, or it might take 1–2 days to complete before the market makes its final, major move. 

Pay close attention to **Mondays, Tuesdays, and Fridays**. These are high-probability days where an indication is set early, and the market spends the rest of the week fulfilling that move. We use the London open to identify if the "Yellow Box" standoff has been settled by the sellers or the buyers.

## 5. Execution: Scaling Down for Entry

Once the 1-hour or Daily timeframe gives us the bearish bias, we scale down to the 5-minute or 15-minute charts to find the entry.

1.  **Establish the Bias:** If the 1-hour structure is bearish (Lower Highs/Lower Lows), we only look for sell setups.
2.  **Wait for the Correction:** We don't chase the move. We wait for the 5-minute or 15-minute timeframe to "correct" (pull back) into a level of 1-hour resistance.
3.  **Identify the Shift:** The entry happens when that 5-minute micro-structure—which was temporarily bullish during the correction—breaks its own "Higher Low" and turns bearish to align with the 1-hour trend.

**The Entry Method:**
*   **The Early/Risky Way:** Enter as soon as the 5-minute structure shifts from bullish to bearish near a 1-hour resistance level. Place your stop-loss above that new 5-minute Lower High.
*   **The Safe Way:** Wait for price to break the major 1-hour support level. Once that "floor" is gone, the sellers are confirmed.

## 6. Trade Management and Exit Strategies

As a professional mentor, I don’t aim for small scalps. I want you to trade the trend. My personal preference is a firm **Risk-to-Reward (RR) of 1:4**.

### Exit and Take-Profit Philosophy
*   **Alert-Based Management:** I don’t always use a "Hard TP" set on the platform. Instead, I set **alerts** at previous swing lows on the 1-hour chart. When the alert hits, I monitor the reaction. If buyers are stepping in, I take partial profits.
*   **The "Micro-Structure" Trail:** To maximize a trend, follow the 5-minute or 15-minute lower highs. As long as the market continues to print Lower Highs, stay in the trade. You only exit when that micro-structure breaks (i.e., price makes a new Higher High).

**Note for Beginners:** I recommend trailing your stop-loss to lock in profits early. It helps manage the emotional side of trading. As you get more experienced, you’ll learn to hold for those 1-hour swing targets.

## 7. Advanced Tips: Avoiding Noise and Liquidity Traps

Success in these markets requires the discipline to ignore the "noise" and wait for high-probability volume.

> **Trade with Volume**
> By sticking strictly to the London session open, you avoid the choppy consolidation of the off-sessions. High volume is what allows price to break through "Yellow Box" standoffs cleanly without fakeouts.

> **Higher Timeframe Priority**
> Always trust your **Daily and 1-hour structures** over 5-minute noise. If the 1-hour is bearish, a 5-minute "liquidity grab" to the upside is just a better opportunity to sell, not a reason to flip your bias.

> **Accepting Losses in Consolidation**
> Even with a perfect markup, some sessions—like one we recently saw on GU—will simply consolidate and result in a loss. If the market stays flat despite being under a key level, that's just part of the game. Take the loss, stay disciplined, and return the next day when the volume is there.
