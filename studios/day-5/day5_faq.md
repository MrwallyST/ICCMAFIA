# FAQ: Day 5 ICC Trading Course - Understanding Entries and Market Structure

### 1. Foundational Concepts of Market Structure

**How does market structure define "Highs" and "Lows" in the context of buyer/seller control?**  
In this framework, a **High** is not just a peak on a chart; it is the specific price point where sellers are actively holding the market from going any higher. It represents the limit of buyer strength. A **Low** is the point where buyers step in with enough force to drive price back up because they see value.

> "At this level buyers have the most control and it’s like a brick wall in a sense price is price has a brick wall and it’s not letting it go any lower than this."

**What is the fundamental rule for determining potential bullishness or bearishness?**  
To establish your market bias, you must adhere to these two golden formulas:

*   `Anything above a swing low = potential bullishness`
*   `Anything below a swing high = potential bearishness`

### 2. Reaction Levels and Identifying Momentum

**How do "Reaction Levels" help identify when sellers are no longer in control?**  
Reaction levels show you the shift in power. You look at a previous High where sellers were "sitting" and trying to drive price down. When price breaks out and establishes a new High, it proves that the sellers at that level have been completely swept out by a surge of new buyers. The "boss" (the seller level) has been defeated, and the market is now reacting to a new environment controlled by buyers.

**What is "Liquidity" in simple terms, and why does the market move toward it?**  
Liquidity is simply "grabbing more money." The market is a predator; it doesn't want you to win—it wants to collect the capital you lose to fund its own movements.

The author uses the **Chanel bag analogy**: If you see a luxury bag you can’t afford, you have to go find more money before you can buy it. When a new, more expensive bag is released, you must find even more capital. The market works the same way. It sees a higher price level (the bag) it wants to reach, but it needs more capital to get there. To "afford" the move, it liquidates existing traders—hitting their stop losses and taking their money—to collect the fuel necessary to push higher.

### 3. Entry Execution and Stop Loss Placement

**At what specific point should a trader initiate a bullish entry?**  
The entry must occur exactly where the bullish momentum initiates. To find this, you must scale down to the **15-minute time frame**. As soon as price is above the identified seller level and the volume kicks in, that is your entry. You are trading the reaction and the immediate momentum, not a guess.

**Where should the stop loss be placed to protect a position?**  
The stop loss **must** be placed at the level of the "higher low" created by the trend. This is a non-negotiable fundamental of an uptrend. If price breaks that higher low, the structural integrity of your trade is gone, and you must be out.

**Which time frames are most critical for identifying key swing highs and lows?**  
The 1-hour, 4-hour, and Daily time frames are your primary tools. Specifically, the **1-hour and 4-hour** are the keys. If you use these time frames to identify your swings, it will fundamentally change your results by removing the insignificance of lower-tier fluctuations.

### 5. Psychological Pitfalls and Error Correction

**What are the three main psychological traps that lead to consistent losses?**  
1.  **FOMO (Fear of Missing Out):** Chasing a breakout because you’re afraid the move will leave without you, rather than waiting for clear structural direction.
2.  **Greed:** The urgent, desperate need to get money *now*, which leads to trading every minor movement without a plan.
3.  **Revenge Trading:** Trying to "win back" money immediately after being stopped out by a seller surge or liquidation move.

**How does the author's view of "Break and Retest" differ from conventional retail trading?**  
The author rejects the retail version of "break and retest" because it is often "fake news." Traditional retail teaches that price should return perfectly to the broken support/resistance. In reality, the market often "liquidates everything," intentionally moving *below* that retest level to stop out retail traders before continuing the move. If you follow the retail textbook, you will be liquidated by these "fake moves" designed to steal your capital.
