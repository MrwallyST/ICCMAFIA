### 2. The Fundamental Laws of Highs and Lows

In this strategy, we treat price levels as a **"Brick Wall."** These aren't just lines on a chart; they are barriers of power shifts where the market physically cannot proceed without a massive injection of volume.

*   **A "High" (The Sellers' Fortress):** This is the highest point price reaches before sellers aggressively defend their territory to drive the price down. Once a high is established, selling potential remains active at that level until it is decisively broken.
*   **A "Low" (The Buyers' Floor):** This is the lowest point price reaches before buyers step in, deciding the asset is undervalued and driving the price back up.

**Market Bias Identification**

| Condition | Potential Outcome | Required Timeframe |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Price is above a Swing Low | Potential Bullishness | 1-Hour / 4-Hour / Daily |
| Price is below a Swing High | Potential Bearishness | 1-Hour / 4-Hour / Daily |

*Note: If price is under a previous high, do not look for buys. Sellers are still in control until that "Brick Wall" is dismantled.*

### 3. Understanding Liquidity: The "Chanel Bag" Analogy

Liquidity is just a fancy word for "grabbing more money." You need to understand that when you make money, you are essentially "stealing" it from the market. The market has no intention of letting you win; it wants to collect your money to fuel its next move.

**The Chanel Bag Analogy:**
Imagine you see a Chanel bag you want, but you’re short on cash. You go out, work, and collect more money so you can return and buy it. Then, a newer, more expensive bag comes out. Again, you must leave the store, collect more money, and return to make the purchase.

The market does the exact same thing. Before it makes a major move toward a level of sellers, it needs to **liquidate** the retail traders. It seeks out your stop-losses because it needs that "collected money" to move price to the next level. It intentionally triggers your loss to fund its own journey.

### 4. The Psychological Trap: FOMO, Greed, and Revenge Trading

You will fail if you keep chasing money instead of logic. The market is designed to exploit your lack of discipline during two specific moves.

> **⚠️ WARNING: THE PSYCHOLOGICAL TRAP**
>
> *   **FOMO & Greed (The First Push):** This occurs during the **initial breakout**. Traders see a green candle and panic, thinking they’re missing the move. They jump in blindly without waiting for direction.
> *   **Revenge Trading (The Correction):** This occurs during the **move down**. After being stopped out of the FOMO trade, traders get angry and try to "get back" at the market by shorting it, right as the market is actually preparing for its real move.
>
> **The Logic Solution:** The **"Second Push"** is the entry for professionals. While the retail sheep are revenge trading the correction, the disciplined trader waits for the market to liquidate the weak hands and show its true intent.

### 6. Tactical Application: Finding the Entry

Follow this process with zero deviations. We analyze on the **1-Hour** and execute on the **15-Minute**.

1.  **Identify the High/Low Area:** Mark the highest and lowest points on the 1-hour chart.
2.  **Analyze the "Break":** Look for the sharp moves that "broke something." Did the move break a previous structure? If it didn't break anything, it’s not a move worth trading.
3.  **Wait for the Liquidation:** Let the first move (FOMO) and the correction (Revenge) happen. Wait for the sellers to be "swept out."
4.  **Scale Down to the 15-Minute:** Once the 1-hour structure is clear, move to the 15-minute chart to pinpoint the entry.
5.  **Identify Momentum Initiation:** Your entry is the exact moment price breaks back above the level where sellers were previously in control.

**The Golden Rule:** Do not trade under a previous high. Under that level, selling potential is still active. You only enter once price proves that sellers are no longer in control.
