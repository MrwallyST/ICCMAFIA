## The Fundamentals of Reaction Levels
In this game, market structure is your map, and reaction levels are the **"Brick Walls."** A High isn't just a point on a chart; it is a ceiling where sellers are actively defending their territory. A Low is a floor where buyers are holding the line. Understanding who is being "swept out" at these levels is the difference between a professional trader and a retail statistic.

### Market Dynamics at Key Levels
| Level Type | Participant & Market Action |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Swing High** | **Sellers’ Territory:** Acts as a price ceiling. Sellers exert downward pressure to protect their interests, driving price away from the peak. |
| **Swing Low** | **Buyers’ Territory:** Acts as a price floor. Buyers step into the market at these "cheap" levels to absorb selling volume and drive price upward. |

## Decoding Market Liquidity: The "Chanel Bag" Analogy
Liquidity isn't a complex mystery; it's just the market "grabbing more money" to afford its next move. 

The market sees a major move it wants to make but lacks the necessary volume (capital). Think of it like a shopper who sees a Chanel bag they can't quite afford. They don't just give up; they go out, acquire the cash, and then return to the store to make the purchase. The market does the same thing. It "goes out" to hit retail stop-losses—liquidating your cash—to fuel its actual intended trend. 

**Remember:** When you make money from the markets, you are actually stealing from it. The market doesn't want you to steal; it wants you to lose so it can collect your capital for its own moves.

## Psychology of the Retail Trader: FOMO and Greed
Most retail traders fail because they are blinded by greed and a mindset of deficiency. They trade because they *need* money today, rather than trading the reality of the chart.

1.  **The Breakout Trap (FOMO & Greed):** Amateurs chase the first impulsive candle of a breakout. They fear missing the move and enter prematurely, usually right before the market "goes out for cash" (the liquidity sweep).
2.  **The Revenge Trade:** After being liquidated by the initial sweep, the retail trader gets angry. They enter a "revenge" position out of deficiency, often selling right as the market is establishing the higher low for a massive buy.

## The ICC Entry Framework (Review & Application)
The ICC framework ensures you are trading with momentum rather than betting against a "Brick Wall" that hasn't crumbled yet.

**The Three Stages of a Structural Shift:**
1.  **Indication:** This is a "sweep of control." Price breaks a previous high or low, physically removing the opposing participants from their positions. It shows the "wall" has been breached.
2.  **Correction:** This is the liquidity hunt. Price retraces to grab the money it needs to "afford" the next leg of the move.
3.  **Continuation:** This is where the momentum officially kicks back in.

**Core Execution Rules:**
*   **The "Under-High" Rule:** If you are seeking a buy, **never** trade while price is still under a previous high. That level still has selling potential. Trading there is betting against a wall that is still standing. Wait for price to regain momentum *above* the level where sellers lost control.
*   **Entry Point:** Initiate your trade only at the specific point where bullish (or bearish) momentum resumes after the correction.
*   **Stop Loss Placement:** Your stop loss must be placed at the higher low (for buys) or lower high (for sells) established during the correction phase. This is where buyers "held the line" against the liquidation attempt.
