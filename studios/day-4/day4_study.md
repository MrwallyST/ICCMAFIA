# Continuations & The Entry Model: Mastering the Final Phase

This study guide provides a comprehensive breakdown of Day 4 of the Trades by Sci ICC curriculum. It covers the third and final phase of the ICC Framework — The Continuation — and the precise, multi-timeframe entry model used to execute high-probability institutional trades safely and profitably.

---

## 1. The Grand Finale: What is a Continuation?

### The ICC Cycle Revisited
The entire ICC Framework builds to this moment. After the market has shown its Indication (the directional signal) and has run its Correction (the liquidity grab that traps retail traders), the Continuation is the payoff — the moment the market resumes its original direction and price travels to its true target.

**The Profitable Market Cycle:**
`Indication ➡️ Correction ➡️ Continuation ➡️ Repeat`

This cycle is the heartbeat of every liquid market. Mastering the ability to identify where you are in this cycle is the difference between guessing and trading with a structured edge.

### Defining the Continuation
A Continuation occurs at the precise moment the correction phase ends. The specific triggers are:
- **Price stops making new lows** (in a bullish trend) or **new highs** (in a bearish trend) during the correction.
- **The market "flips"**: price structure shifts back in the direction of the original Indication.
- **A new push begins** that aims to exceed the original Indication's high/low and reach the massive 1-Hour structural target.

The Continuation is the "second time around" — the move that the patient, rule-following trader has been waiting for while retail traders were already stopped out.

## 2. The Surgeon Analogy: Multi-Timeframe Mastery

This is the most important concept in Day 4. To execute a Continuation trade precisely, you must think like a surgeon — using different instruments for different levels of precision.

### The X-Ray: The 1-Hour (and 4-Hour) Chart
The 1-Hour or 4-Hour chart is your diagnostic tool. It tells you:
- What the overall trend direction is (Uptrend or Downtrend)
- Where the Indication occurred (the structural break that started the move)
- The "massive" levels — the key highs or lows that represent your Take Profit target

Think of a patient who says, "I have a headache." The 1-Hour chart tells you there IS a problem and gives you the general direction of where to look. You would not perform surgery based only on this information.

### The Scalpel: The 5-Minute (and 15-Minute) Chart
After identifying the macro setup on the 1-Hour chart, you zoom down to the 5-Minute (or 15-Minute) chart. This chart reveals:
- The micro-structure of the Correction — every small HL, LH, and pivot
- The exact moment the Correction is ending and the market is "flipping" back
- The specific price level where the second push begins (your entry trigger)

The surgeon does not guess where to cut based on the X-Ray alone. He goes deep inside with precision instruments. As a trader, the 5-Minute chart is your scalpel — you use it to cut into the 1-Hour trend and find the exact moment to enter.

### The Two-Step Process
1. **Step 1 (1H Chart):** Identify the Indication and Correction on the 1-Hour chart. Know your target.
2. **Step 2 (5m Chart):** Drop to the 5-Minute chart. Watch the Correction form its structure. Wait for the second push past your key level. Enter.

## 3. The Execution Blueprint

### The Second Time Around Rule
This is the cardinal rule of Continuation entries:

> **Never trade the first breakout of a level. Always wait for the second time price pushes past it.**

Here is why this rule exists:
- The first push past a level is often a **liquidity grab** — it triggers the stop-losses of retail traders who went short at that level, fueling a brief spike.
- After the first push, the market pulls back (the Correction), forming a new structure (a Lower High in a bullish move).
- The **second push** past that key level on the 5-Minute chart is the confirmation that the liquidity has been cleared and the real institutional move is beginning.

### Step-by-Step Execution
1. **Identify the macro setup** on the 1H chart. Mark the Indication level and the 1H target.
2. **Drop to the 5-minute chart** and observe the Correction forming. Watch for LH and HL structure.
3. **Wait for the Correction to stall**. Price will stop making new aggressive lows (for a long trade).
4. **Identify your key level** on the 5-Minute chart — this is where you expect the second push.
5. **Enter on the second push past that level**, not the first.
6. **Place Stop Loss** safely above the previous Lower High (or below the previous Higher Low) created during the Correction.
7. **Set Take Profit** at the massive 1-Hour level where the original Indication started.

## 4. Stop Loss & Take Profit Placement

### Stop Loss: Structurally Protected
Your Stop Loss must be placed **beyond the most recent structural point created during the Correction**. This means:
- For a long (buy) trade: Stop Loss goes **below the Higher Low** that was formed during the correction on the 5-minute chart.
- For a short (sell) trade: Stop Loss goes **above the Lower High** that was formed during the correction on the 5-minute chart.

This placement is safe because if price breaks that structural level, the setup is no longer valid — the Correction is not over, and the market is doing something else entirely.

### Take Profit: The Massive 1-Hour Level
Your Take Profit target is **not arbitrary**. It is the massive 1-Hour structural level where:
- Price is anticipated to create a **brand new High** (in an uptrend)
- Or a **brand new Low** (in a downtrend)

This is the natural destination of the market cycle — the original "Indication" implied this move, the "Correction" funded it by grabbing liquidity, and the "Continuation" delivers price to its intended destination.

## 7. Glossary of Key Terms

- **Continuation:** Step 3 of ICC. The resumption of the original trend after the Correction is over and liquidity has been cleared.
- **The Market Cycle:** The repeating sequence of Indication → Correction → Continuation that governs institutional price movement.
- **The X-Ray (1H Chart):** The higher timeframe used to identify the overall direction, the Indication, and the Take Profit target.
- **The Scalpel (5m Chart):** The lower timeframe used to zoom into the Correction and pinpoint the exact entry moment.
- **The Second Time Around:** The rule that a trader only enters on the second push past a key level, never the first breakout.
- **Stop Loss:** A pre-set exit order placed beyond the most recent structural point of the Correction to limit risk.
- **Take Profit:** A pre-set exit order placed at the massive 1-Hour structural target — a new High or Low.
- **Liquidity Grab:** The market's mechanism of pushing past a level to trigger retail stop-losses before reversing in the true direction.
- **Lower High (LH):** A peak formed during a Correction in a bullish trend, used as the reference point for Stop Loss placement.
- **Higher Low (HL):** A trough formed during a Correction in a bearish trend, used as the reference point for Stop Loss placement.
