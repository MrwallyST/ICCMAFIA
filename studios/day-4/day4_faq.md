1. Why shouldn't I trade the first breakout?
The first breakout is often a liquidity grab or "fake-out" designed to trap eager retail traders. Waiting for the correction and trading the "Second Time Around" ensures the market has finished grabbing liquidity and is safely moving in your direction.

2. What timeframes should I use for this model?
According to the Surgeon Analogy, you use the 1-Hour (or 4-Hour) chart as your X-Ray to find the overall setup/Indication. You use the 5-Minute (or 15-Minute) chart as your Scalpel to pinpoint the exact entry when the correction ends.

3. Where exactly does the Stop Loss go?
Once you execute on the 5-minute continuation, place your Stop Loss safely above the previous peak (the lower high) or below the previous valley (the higher low) that was created *during* the correction phase.

4. How do I know when the Continuation has actually started?
The continuation starts when price, after forming a correction, pushes past your key structural level on the 5-minute chart for the second time, flipping back into the original trend's direction.

5. What is my Take Profit target?
Your Take Profit target is the massive 1-Hour level where the original Indication started, anticipating the market will sweep that liquidity and create a brand new high or low.
