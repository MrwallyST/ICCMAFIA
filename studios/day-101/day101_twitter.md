## 2. The Dual Directions of Market Movement
Trading isn't as complicated as people want you to believe. Price only moves in two primary directions:
*   **Uptrend**
*   **Downtrend**

Understanding these directions is the essence of **Market Structure**. This is your framework, your foundation, and your ultimate cheat code for every trade you will ever take.

## 4. The First Mission: Finding Your Anchor Points
Before you can identify a trend, you must find your starting points. You cannot have a "Higher High" or a "Lower Low" if you have nothing to compare them to. 

When you open a chart, your **First Mission** is to identify the current **High (H)** and **Low (L)**.
*   **Identifying the High (H):** Look for where the price was pushing up (green candles) and then rejected heavily, followed by a shift into red candles. That highest point of rejection is your H.
*   **Identifying the Low (L):** Look for where the price was selling off and then hit a floor, followed by a strong buying move. That lowest point is your L.

**The Range Warning:** If price is stuck between your identified H and L without breaking out, you are in a **Range**. Do not attempt to identify a trend or take trades while price is stuck in this zone. You must wait for the breakout to establish the next move.

## 5. Technical Breakdown: Anatomy of Trends
Once you have your anchor points (H and L), you can begin to identify the trend.

### Uptrend: The Climb
An uptrend is established when price breaks above the previous high. It consists of:
*   **Higher Highs (HH):** Price exceeds the previous peak.
*   **Higher Lows (HL):** Price pulls back but finds support higher than the previous bottom.

### Downtrend: The Slide
A downtrend is established when price breaks below the previous low. It consists of:
*   **Lower Highs (LH):** Price pulls up but fails to reach the previous peak.
*   **Lower Lows (LL):** Price drops and exceeds the previous bottom.

## 6. The Bread and Butter Rule
Price movement is cyclical. In a trending market, you can use the "Rule of Expectation" to predict the next move with high confidence.

| Current Move | Expected Next Move |
| :--- | :--- |
| Higher Low (HL) | Higher High (HH) |
| Higher High (HH) | Higher Low (HL) |
| Lower High (LH) | Lower Low (LL) |
| Lower Low (LL) | Lower High (LH) |

**Note:** The higher the time frame, the more price is forced to respect this rule. Small time frames are messy; the 1H, 4H, and Daily are where this rule becomes law.

## 7. Strategic Entry Criteria
We don't just guess where to enter; we enter based on the Rule of Expectation. 

*   **Buy Trades:** We enter at the **Higher Low (HL)**. Why? Because the Bread and Butter Rule dictates that a Higher Low *must* lead to a Higher High.
*   **Sell Trades:** We enter at the **Lower High (LH)**. Why? Because the rule dictates that a Lower High *must* lead to a Lower Low.

By entering at the HL or LH, you are positioning yourself at the start of the next major expansion move.
