# Trading Fundamentals: Market Structure and Trend Identification

This study guide provides a comprehensive overview of the foundational principles of day trading as outlined in the "Day Trading Crash Course." It focuses on the identification of trends, the mechanics of market structure, and the significance of time frames in technical analysis.

---

## Core Concepts Summary

### 1. The Definition of Market Structure
Market structure is the primary "cheat code" for traders. It is the organized movement of price on a chart, characterized by trends. Understanding market structure allows a trader to predict potential price movements because the structure is more reliable and breaks less frequently on higher time frames.

### 2. Directions of Price Movement
Price moves in two primary directions:
*   **Uptrend:** Characterized by price moving upward, creating a sequence of higher peaks and troughs.
*   **Downtrend:** Characterized by price moving downward, creating a sequence of lower peaks and troughs.

### 3. The Rule of Time Frames
The reliability of market structure is directly tied to the time frame being analyzed. 
*   **Higher Time Frames (HTF):** The 1-hour (1H), 4-hour (4H), and 1-day (Daily) charts are considered the standard for identifying strong market structure. These time frames are the most respected and do not break fast.
*   **Lower Time Frames:** Smaller time frames are less likely to respect the rules of market structure and are prone to more frequent "breaks" or noise.

### 4. Identifying Trends and Entry Points
Trends are identified by comparing current price points to previous ones. A trend cannot be confirmed if the price is stuck in a **Range** (a sideways movement with no clear progression).
*   **Buying (Long Positions):** Traders look for entries at **Higher Lows (HL)** during an uptrend.
*   **Selling (Short Positions):** Traders look for entries at **Lower Highs (LH)** during a downtrend.

### 5. The Rule of Repetition
Market structure follows a repetitive pattern. Once a specific point is established, the market seeks to create the next logical point in the sequence:
*   If price makes a **Higher Low (HL)**, it is expected to make a **Higher High (HH)**.
*   If price makes a **Higher High (HH)**, it is expected to make a **Higher Low (HL)**.
*   If price makes a **Lower High (LH)**, it is expected to make a **Lower Low (LL)**.
*   If price makes a **Lower Low (LL)**, it is expected to make a **Lower High (LH)**.

## Glossary of Important Terms

| Term | Definition |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Market Structure** | The framework of price movement on a chart, consisting of trends and patterns that serve as a foundational guide for trading. |
| **Uptrend** | A market condition where price is consistently moving higher, marked by Higher Highs and Higher Lows. |
| **Downtrend** | A market condition where price is consistently moving lower, marked by Lower Highs and Lower Lows. |
| **Higher High (HH)** | A peak in price that is higher than the previous peak. |
| **Higher Low (HL)** | A trough in price that is higher than the previous trough; the primary entry point for buy trades. |
| **Lower High (LH)** | A peak in price that is lower than the previous peak; the primary entry point for sell trades. |
| **Lower Low (LL)** | A trough in price that is lower than the previous trough. |
| **Higher Time Frame (HTF)** | Charts representing longer durations (1-hour, 4-hour, Daily) used to identify the strongest and most reliable trends. |
| **Range** | A period where price is stuck between two levels without breaking out, resulting in no identifiable higher highs or lower lows. |
| **Pivot Point Indicator** | A technical tool that can be used to help identify and mark significant highs and lows on a price chart. |
| **Bullish Movement** | An upward surge in price, often indicated by a sequence of green candles. |
| **Rejection** | A sharp change in price direction, such as when price hits a high point and immediately begins to sell off (turning from green candles to red). |
