### 3. Decoding the Two Directions: Uptrends and Downtrends
The market only moves in two directions that matter for your bank account: up and down. Identifying the environment is your first step to getting paid.

#### 3.1 The Uptrend (Buying Environment)
An uptrend is characterized by a sequence of **Higher Highs (HH)** and **Higher Lows (HL)**. 
*   **The Comparison Rule:** You don’t have a trend until you have something to compare it to. You cannot call a point a Higher High until price has officially broken above the previous high.
*   **The Entry Nuance:** A Higher Low is not confirmed until you "get something out of it"—meaning the price must move up and create a new Higher High.
*   **Action Plan:** 
    *   In an uptrend, you are looking for **buy trades** only.
    *   Your entry points occur at the **Higher Lows (HL)**.

#### 3.2 The Downtrend (Selling Environment)
A downtrend is defined by **Lower Highs (LH)** and **Lower Lows (LL)**.
*   **The Logic:** Price breaks a previous low (creating a Lower Low) and then pulls back but fails to exceed the previous high (creating a Lower High).
*   **Action Plan:**
    *   In a downtrend, you are looking for **sell trades** only.
    *   Your entry points occur at the **Lower Highs (LH)**.

#### 3.3 The No-Trade Zone: Ranges
If price is "stuck in a range," you are in a No-Trade Zone. Without a clear break of structure, there are no highs or lows to compare. You cannot take any trades until price finally breaks out of one side or the other. Don't get chopped up in the noise—stay out until the structure is clear.

### 6. Practical Application: Training Your Eye
When you open a chart, your **"First Mission"** is to establish a baseline. You cannot identify a trend until you find your initial **High (H)** and **Low (L)**. 

#### Identifying the Shift
To find a High, look for a string of green candles followed by a "whole shift" to red candles. That highest point before the sell-off is your **High (H)**. Conversely, the lowest point before a strong bullish buy-up is your **Low (L)**. You must establish these two points before you can even think about labeling a Higher High or Lower Low.
