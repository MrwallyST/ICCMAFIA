# Blog Post: The "1 to 3 Session" Rule for Clean Chart Markups

If you open your trading platform right now and your chart is covered in horizontal lines from the Daily timeframe, the 4-Hour timeframe, and the 1-Hour timeframe... you have a problem. 

You are suffering from Analysis Paralysis. When you have too many levels marked, every single candle looks like it's hitting "resistance." You hesitate. You doubt your strategy. You miss the real moves.

In Day 10 of the TradesBySci curriculum, we fix this by completely purging the noise.

## Step 1: Clear the Chart

The first step is simple: delete everything. You need a fresh start. Market structure (higher highs, lower lows) functions exactly the same way on every timeframe. You do not need a messy chart to see it.

## Step 2: The 1 to 3 Session Rule

Retail traders often scroll back weeks into the past to find a level of support. The truth is, the most actionable institutional liquidity and volume are found in the immediate past. 

TradesBySci introduces the **1 to 3 Session Rule**: You only need to look at the price action from the last one to three trading sessions (e.g., the last Asian, London, and New York sessions). 

## Step 3: Finding Swings with Common Sense

Within those last 3 sessions, look for the extreme points. Where did price push aggressively, stop abruptly, and reverse? 

Use common sense: "If price came all the way here, was really bullish, but stopped and turned around... that must mean sellers are here." That is your valid Swing High. 

*Pro Tip:* If you are struggling to spot these swings, temporarily turn on the "Pivot Points High and Low" indicator to test your eyes and verify your markings.

## Step 4: The No Trade Zone

Once you have your Swing High and Swing Low marked from the recent sessions, the area between them is restricted. This is the **No Trade Zone**. You must have the discipline to sit on your hands and do nothing while price chops around in this area.

## Step 5: Wait for the Indication

Eventually, price will break out of the No Trade Zone—it will break the swing high or the swing low. This break is your **Indication**. 

However, as per the ICC framework, you NEVER trade the Indication. The breakout is often a trap to grab liquidity. You wait for the market to correct, and you execute on the Continuation only when a major session (like the New York 9:30 AM open) steps in with volume to confirm the move.

Keep your charts clean, trust the recent sessions, and stop overcomplicating your analysis.
