# Frequently Asked Questions: Marking Up Charts (Day 10)

**Q: Why shouldn't I mark levels from the Daily or Weekly timeframes for day trading?**
A: Marking too many high-timeframe levels causes "analysis paralysis." Your chart becomes a mess of lines, making it impossible to read current, actionable structure. Market structure rules apply universally; you only need the most recent levels to make day trading decisions.

**Q: What is the 1 to 3 Session Rule?**
A: Instead of looking back weeks, you only look back at the price action from the last 1 to 3 trading sessions (e.g., the most recent Asian, London, and New York sessions). The swing highs and lows formed in this recent window contain the immediate liquidity institutions are targeting.

**Q: What is a "No Trade Zone"?**
A: Once you identify the recent Swing High and Swing Low, the entire area between those two points is the No Trade Zone. You must sit on your hands and avoid trading until price officially breaks out of this range (giving you an Indication).

**Q: I struggle to identify valid Swing Highs and Lows. What can I do?**
A: TradesBySci recommends using "common sense"—look for where an aggressive push in price abruptly stopped and reversed. If you need technical help, temporarily turn on the "Pivot Points High and Low" indicator in TradingView to train your eyes.

**Q: If price breaks out of the No Trade Zone, should I enter a trade immediately?**
A: Absolutely not. The initial breakout is the "Indication" in the ICC framework. If you trade it, you are trading a breakout, which is often a trap. Wait for the Correction (pullback) and then enter on the Continuation when a major session provides volume.
