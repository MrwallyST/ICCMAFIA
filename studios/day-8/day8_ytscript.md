# Analysis of the Trades by Sci (ICC) Trading Strategy

## Executive Summary

The Trades by Sci methodology, specifically the **ICC (Indication, Correction, Continuation)** framework, is a price-action-based trading strategy designed to capitalize on market trends while avoiding common retail pitfalls such as FOMO (fear of missing out) and liquidity traps. The system rejects the use of traditional indicators, instead focusing on market structure, liquidity grabs, and timeframe correlation. 

The core philosophy posits that the market moves in a predictable three-step cycle fueled by the emotional responses of retail traders. By identifying an "Indication" (breakout), waiting for a "Correction" (liquidity grab), and entering on a "Continuation" (trend resumption), a trader can shift from chasing explosive moves to executing high-probability, strategic entries. Success in this system requires extreme patience, strict adherence to higher timeframe structures, and a realistic approach to capital management.

---

## The ICC Framework: A Three-Step Approach

The ICC strategy is a systematic "rule book" used to determine entries, exits, and stop-loss levels. It is built on the premise that the initial breakout is often a trap designed to collect liquidity before the real trend begins.

### 1. Indication
An Indication occurs when price breaks above or below a significant swing level (high or low) on a higher timeframe, such as the 1-hour or 4-hour chart.
*   **Purpose:** It acts as a "blueprint" or "information," signaling the market’s intended direction and showing where momentum and volume are originating.
*   **Key Rule:** An Indication is **not** an entry signal. It is a sign that a trend is starting or pushing in a specific direction.

### 2. Correction
The Correction is a calculated pause or reversal that follows a major breakout.
*   **Purpose:** To "shake out" emotional traders and grab their money (liquidity) to fuel the sustainable trend. 
*   **The Coke Bottle Analogy:**
    *   **Shakeup:** Consolidation where pressure builds.
    *   **Explosion:** The Indication (cap flies off).
    *   **The Mess:** The Correction (fizz goes everywhere). This "mess" must be cleaned up before the move continues.
*   **Psychology:** Markets target FOMO traders who jump in late at the top or early breakout traders with tight stop-losses.

### 3. Continuation
The Continuation is the entry phase where price, after finishing its correction, flips its internal structure back to align with the higher timeframe trend.
*   **Entry Signal:** Price breaks back above the level established by the Indication after successfully clearing out liquidity.
*   **Validation:** Confirmed when the lower timeframe (5-minute or 15-minute) structure aligns with the 1-hour/4-hour bearish or bullish bias.

## Market Dynamics and Structure

### Identifying Trends
The foundation of the strategy is the ability to identify the current market state.

| Market State | Definition | Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Uptrend** | Characterized by Higher Highs (HH) and Higher Lows (HL). | Look for buy opportunities. |
| **Downtrend** | Characterized by Lower Lows (LL) and Lower Highs (LH). | Look for sell opportunities. |
| **Consolidation**| Price moves sideways, breaking both highs and lows inconsistently. | **Stay away.** Avoid trading "rule-breakers." |

### The Role of Liquidity
Liquidity is defined simply as "money in the market." Highs and lows are protected by "brick walls" of buyers and sellers. When these levels are broken, it costs significant capital. The market often liquidates traders by hitting stop-losses located just beyond these swing levels. 
*   **Market Sentiment:** If price is above a swing low, it has bullish potential. If it is below a swing high, it has bearish potential.
*   **The "Trap":** Breakouts often induce FOMO; the market then reverses to collect those traders' stops before continuing.

## Timeframe Correlation and Strategy Execution

Successful execution requires aligning multiple timeframes to ensure "confluence."

*   **Higher Timeframes (1-Hour / 4-Hour):** Used for the "Satellite View." This is where swing levels are marked, the overall trend is identified, and the Indication is spotted. These levels are stronger and hold more "buying and selling pressure."
*   **Lower Timeframes (5-Minute / 15-Minute):** Used as the "Microscope." Traders scale down to these timeframes during the Correction phase to monitor for a change in internal structure (e.g., a 15-minute downtrend flipping to an uptrend) to signal the Continuation.

### Steps for Entry (The Checklist)
1.  **Spot Indication:** Identify a new HH or LL on the 1-hour or 4-hour chart.
2.  **Define Information:** Label the Indication as information, not an entry.
3.  **Anticipate Correction:** Wait for the "mess" to begin as price pulls back.
4.  **Monitor on 15m/5m:** Watch for the lower timeframe to lose momentum in its counter-trend move (e.g., look for equal highs or a failure to make a new low).
5.  **Execute Continuation:** Enter when the lower timeframe structure breaks back in favor of the original higher timeframe trend. Target the previous high/low or a "final boss" level of liquidity.

## Psychology and Risk Management

### Capital and Asset Selection
The strategy emphasizes trading assets with high volatility and volume, specifically:
*   **NASDAQ (NAS100)**
*   **Gold (XAUUSD)**
*   **US30**

**Note on Capital:** The strategy discourages attempting to grow very small accounts (e.g., $50). It is advised to work a job to build a "tight space" capital of $1,000–$5,000 before attempting to trade full-time. The goal is consistent gains, not gambling for "crystal ball" predictions.

### Mindset Shifts
*   **Stop Being a "Genie":** Do not try to predict what the market *will* do; follow what it *is* doing.
*   **Patience is Profit:** Once the plan is visible, the only thing between the trader and profit is the patience to wait for the setup to complete.
*   **Avoid the "Noise":** Higher timeframes eliminate the distractions of social media "scalping" and focus on government/economic-driven trends.

## Actionable Insights

*   **Marking Up:** Only use the 1-hour or 4-hour charts to find swing highs and lows. Do not build structure on the 5-minute chart.
*   **Targeting:** When buying, take profits into the sellers (previous highs). When selling, take profits into the buyers (previous lows).
*   **Safety via Continuation:** Avoid trading breakouts directly. Waiting for the second move back into the trend (the Continuation) saves traders from "liquidity grabs" and "fakeouts."
*   **Stop-Loss Placement:** Stop-losses should generally be placed above/below the structural level created by the Correction or the previous structural swing point to allow the trade "room to breathe."
*   **Trading Sessions:** Prioritize high-volume sessions, such as London or New York (specifically around 8:00 AM to 10:30 AM EST), to ensure the Indication is backed by real volume rather than "fake" out-of-session moves.
