# Comprehensive Study Guide: Identifying Reversals

## 1. The Core Concept: Fundamental Structure
The foundation of the ICC framework relies on market structure. A trend must maintain its structure to remain valid.
- **Downtrend:** Must consistently create **Lower Highs (LH)** and **Lower Lows (LL)**.
- **Uptrend:** Must consistently create **Higher Highs (HH)** and **Higher Lows (HL)**.

If price fails to maintain this structure (e.g., it stops making lower highs), the trend is mathematically and psychologically changing.

## 2. The Turning Point (The Indication)
"The moment price breaks a lower high, that's when you need to tell yourself, 'Okay, something's up, price is changing.'"
- **Action:** Instantly stop assuming the previous trend will continue.
- **Mindset:** Be open-minded to the new direction. Do not try to force your previous bias onto the market.

## 3. The 3-Step Process for Reversals (ICC)
You do not trade the exact moment the lower high is broken. That is catching a falling knife. Instead, apply the ICC process:
1. **Indication:** The break of the structural level (e.g., lower high). This is information, not an entry.
2. **Correction:** The market pulls back to retest the broken level. This shakes out the early, emotional buyers and grabs liquidity.
3. **Continuation:** Price rejects the retest and pushes with volume in the new direction. This is the safe entry.

## 4. Session Timing (The Catalyst)
Structural breaks often require institutional volume to be legitimate.
- **London Session:** Often sets up the initial structural break (The Indication).
- **New York Session (9:30 AM Bell):** Often provides the volume for the Retest and Continuation.

## 5. The Golden Rule
Never try to be a genie. Wait for the market to break structure and show you its cards *before* you place a trade. React, don't predict.
