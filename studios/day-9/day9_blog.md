# Blog Post: How to Identify and Trade Reversals Without Catching Falling Knives

Every retail trader has the same fantasy: catching the absolute bottom of a massive crash and riding it all the way to the top. They stare at the chart, see a massive red candle, and think, "It can't possibly go any lower!" They hit buy. The market drops another 50 pips. Their account is blown.

This is called catching a falling knife. And in the ICCMAFIA, we don't play that game.

## Stop Trying to be a Genie

In Day 9 of the TradesBySci curriculum, we tackle the psychology and mechanics of market reversals. The biggest mistake you can make is trying to predict what the market *will* do. Your job is to react to what the market *has already done*.

A trend is defined by its structure. A downtrend must consistently create lower highs and lower lows. As long as those lower highs are respected, the downtrend is valid. You have no business buying.

## The Indication: The First Sign of Change

"The moment price breaks a lower high, that's when you need to tell yourself, 'Okay, something's up... the trend is changing.'" 

This structural break is your **Indication**. It is the market finally showing its cards. It tells you that sellers are losing control and buyers have stepped in with enough volume to break the "brick wall" of the previous high.

## The Retest: Where Amateurs Lose and Professionals Enter

When retail traders see that first lower high break, FOMO kicks in. They buy the breakout immediately at the top of the candle. 

What happens next? The **Correction**.

The market pulls back. It retests the broken level. In doing so, it triggers the stop-losses of all those early breakout buyers. It grabs their liquidity. Once the "mess" is cleaned up, the market uses that liquidity to fuel the real move: The **Continuation**.

## The Gold Trade Example

In the Day 9 lesson, we looked at a live Gold (XAUUSD) trade. The London session pushed price up, creating a new structural high. The Indication was set. 

Instead of jumping in during the quiet pre-market hours, we waited for the New York session. At exactly 9:30 AM EST (the equities open), the volume hit. Price pushed down, retested the 3278 level (Correction), and immediately rejected it, pushing up to 3296 (Continuation). 

That is how you trade a reversal. You don't predict it. You wait for the Indication, you wait for the Correction, and you execute on the Continuation with volume. 

Stop guessing. Start reacting.
