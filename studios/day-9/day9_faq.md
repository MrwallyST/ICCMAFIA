# Frequently Asked Questions: Identifying Reversals (Day 9)

**Q: How do I know if a trend is actually reversing or just pulling back?**
A: You must look at the fundamental structure. In a downtrend, a simple pullback will create a new "Lower High" and then continue dropping. A true reversal begins when price officially breaks ABOVE the previous Lower High. Until that structural break happens, assume the trend is continuing.

**Q: Should I enter a trade as soon as the structural high is broken?**
A: No. In the ICC framework, the initial break of structure is just the "Indication." If you enter immediately, you risk getting caught in the "Correction" phase, where the market pulls back to grab liquidity (hitting early buyers' stop-losses) before the real move happens.

**Q: What timeframe should I use to spot these reversals?**
A: You should identify the overall trend and the initial Indication on a higher timeframe, such as the 1-Hour or 4-Hour chart. Once the Indication is set, scale down to the 15-Minute or 5-Minute chart to monitor the Correction and find your precise entry for the Continuation.

**Q: Why do we care about market sessions for reversals?**
A: Reversals require massive institutional volume to successfully break strong structural levels. If an "Indication" happens during a dead session (like late Asian session), it is often a fakeout. You want to see the Indication or the Continuation align with high-volume events, like the London open or the New York 9:30 AM bell.

**Q: What does "stop trying to be a genie" mean?**
A: It means you should stop trying to predict the exact bottom or top of a market move. You do not have a crystal ball. Wait for the market to actually break structure and show you that the trend has changed, and *then* react to it.
