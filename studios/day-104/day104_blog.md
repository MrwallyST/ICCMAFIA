## 3. Defining Indications vs. Corrections
Market structure is king; a correction is merely a momentary breath before the trend resumes its dominance. To trade this, you must distinguish between a continuation and a retracement using the specific language of price action.

*   **Indications (Trend Continuation):** These occur when price creates **Higher Highs (HH)** in an uptrend or **Lower Lows (LL)** in a downtrend. These are the signals that the current trend has been validated.
*   **Corrections (The Retracement):** These are identified by the creation of **Lower Highs (LH)** or **Higher Lows (HL)**. 

A critical rule for the professional trader: **A correction must never break the overall market structure.** If the 4-hour trend is bullish, the 1-hour and 15-minute charts may temporarily shift bearish to facilitate a retracement, but they must respect the 4-hour support levels to remain valid.

## 4. The Alignment Rule: How to Spot the End of a Correction
The "cheat code" is multi-timeframe alignment. You do not enter a trade because price is "cheap"; you enter because the 4H, 1H, and 15m are once again on the same accord. During this process, many institutional traders look for the **ICC (Institutional Funding Candle)** shift to confirm big-money participation.

### The Alignment Process
1.  **Stagnation at HTF Support:** Watch for the 15-minute price action to reach a 4-hour or 1-hour level of support and go stagnant. This shows the selling pressure is exhausting.
2.  **Neglecting the Lows:** Observe the price "neglecting" or failing to make new lower lows despite the corrective pressure. This failure is the first sign of a trend resumption.
3.  **The Shift in Market Structure (SMS):** The correction is officially dead when the 15-minute chart breaks above the previous internal highs (the Lower Highs of the correction). This is your signal that the 15m has aligned with the 4H bullishness.

**The Golden Rule of Simplicity:** If you find yourself overwhelmed by analysis paralysis, simplify. Monitor the highs. If the 4H and 1H are bullish, wait for the 15m to go bullish. When all three are green, the trade is ready.

## 6. Targeting Hierarchy and Profit Taking
Setting targets is a mathematical exercise in identifying where the next group of opposing participants is located.

*   **The Buyer/Seller Relationship:** In our framework, **Highs = Sellers** and **Lows = Buyers**. When you are in a buy trade, your objective is to ride the momentum into the next cluster of sellers.
*   **The Targeting Hierarchy:**
    *   **Target 1 (Conservative):** The previous 1-hour Lower High.
    *   **Target 2 (Structural):** The previous 4-hour High (the original indication level).
    *   **Target 3 (Trend Extension):** Major 4-hour liquidity/sellers found on even higher horizons.
*   **HTF Superiority:** 4-hour trends are superior because they represent massive volume. These trends can last for days or weeks, allowing you to hold for significant gains while ignoring low-time-frame noise.
