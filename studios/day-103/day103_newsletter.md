# Day Trading Crash Course #3: Mastering Indication, Correction, and Continuation (ICC)

## 1. Foundational Market Structure: The Four Key Points

Successful price action trading is built upon the instant identification of four primary price points. Understanding these points allows a trader to decode the market's intent the moment they open a chart.

*   **Higher High (HH):** A peak that reaches a higher price level than the preceding peak.
*   **Higher Low (HL):** A trough that remains at a higher price level than the preceding trough.
*   **Lower High (LH):** A peak that fails to reach the level of the previous peak.
*   **Lower Low (LL):** A trough that drops below the previous trough.

### Trend Categorization and Establishment
A trend is not established by a single movement. It requires a confirmed sequence of structural points. As a rule, an uptrend is only confirmed when the market produces both a Higher High and a Higher Low.

| Trend Type | Primary Components | Requirement for Establishment |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Uptrend** | Higher Highs (HH) & Higher Lows (HL) | Sequence of HH and HL must align |
| **Downtrend** | Lower Lows (LL) & Lower Highs (LH) | Sequence of LL and LH must align |

*Note: Identifying these points should become a built habit, allowing you to recognize the structural shift from an "indication" to a confirmed trend instantly.*

## 2. The ICC Framework Defined

The ICC acronym represents the lifecycle of a high-probability trade setup. This framework creates a "Rinse and Repeat" cycle that allows swing traders to stay on the right side of momentum.

*   **Indication (I):** The initial signal where the market creates "something new," such as a new high or low. This is the market’s way of revealing its destination.
*   **Correction (C):** The pullback phase. In a bullish move, this is the formation of a Higher Low; in a bearish move, it is the formation of a Lower High.
*   **Continuation (C):** The completion of the move. Price moves back in the direction of the original indication to establish a new higher high or lower low.

### The "Rinse and Repeat" Cycle
*   **Indication (I):** Price tells you where it intends to go.
*   **Correction (C):** Price pulls back to "regain composure."
*   **Continuation (C):** Price executes the move and hits the target.
*   **New Indication (I):** The continuation creates a new high/low, restarting the cycle.

## 3. Identifying the "Indication" and the Entry Level

The **Indication Level** (visualized as a "blue line" on the chart) acts as your primary benchmark. It tells you the market's intent without the need for complex secondary tools.

*   **Price Action over Tools:** You do not need Fibonacci retracements or psychological numbers. If the indication shows price moving from 69,000 toward 73,000, your answer is already provided by the price action itself.
*   **The Entry Trigger (The Gray Area):** While the "blue line" (the indication) represents the breakthrough point or target, your entry is triggered when price moves back above the "gray area"—the previous structure or breakout point where price showed strength before. 

---

## 4. Analyzing the Correction Phase

The correction phase is not a reversal; it is where the market "regains its composure" and "picks up momentum" for the next move.

*   **The "No-Trade" Zone:** If price creates a **Lower High (LH)** during an attempted bullish move, you are in a no-trade zone. This LH indicates a temporary lack of momentum. The setup only becomes valid once price breaks through these internal structural hurdles.
*   **The Second Push:** You need a "second push" around the correction to provide the necessary confidence for entry. This second push confirms that the market has "regained itself." By waiting for this structural confirmation, it becomes extremely difficult to get faked out by minor fluctuations.
*   **Maintaining Composure:** A healthy correction holds its structure. As long as the price stays above the previous Higher Low, the trend is simply "picking up" for the continuation.

## 5. Multi-Timeframe Analysis (MTA) Strategy

To maximize accuracy, you must align the 4-Hour (4H) and 1-Hour (1H) timeframes. The 4H provides the major trend indication, while the 1H acts as the lead indicator for price "picking up."

### MTA Alignment Checklist
1.  **Identify 4H Indication:** Locate a new high or a break above a previous swing high on the 4H chart.
2.  **Execute Timeframe Descent:** Drop to the 1H timeframe to identify internal structural shifts. Look for price to break above the internal 1H lower highs that formed during the correction.
3.  **Confirm Alignment:** Verify that the 1H is breaking back above seller levels while the 4H maintains its structural uptrend. When both timeframes align, the opportunity is high-quality.

*   **Status:** During the London session, the trade experienced a **19k [positional] drawdown**. However, because price remained above the higher low momentum on the 4H, the structure remained "comp" (composed) and valid.

### Gold
The outlook for Gold remains structurally bullish based on the 4H "high-higher low-high" pattern:
*   **Structure:** Gold is currently exhibiting a clear Higher Low formation.
*   **Line in the Sand:** Despite minor corrections that may appear like sell-offs to the untrained eye, the trend remains intact as long as price does not break below the previous Higher Low.

## 7. Risk Management and Invalidation Levels

In price action trading, your "line in the sand" is the point where the market structure is officially broken.

*   **Structural Invalidation:** A bullish trend remains valid as long as price maintains composure above the previous **Higher Low**. If the price breaks below this level, the market's intent has changed, and the trade is invalidated.
*   **Drawdown vs. Loss:** A trade is not "losing" simply because it is in drawdown. It is merely a correction as long as the previous Higher Low is respected.

> **Pro-Tip:** Open a chart and identify the trend in seconds. If you cannot instantly spot the highs and lows, you do not know the market's intent. This reflex is the difference between a novice and a professional strategist.
