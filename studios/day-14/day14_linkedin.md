## 2. The Fundamentals of Swing Points and Chart Markup

A "Swing Point" (High or Low) is a visual **brick wall**. It is the undeniable peak or valley where price hit a limit and pulled a 180 because strong buyers or sellers were sitting there waiting to pounce. 

### Instructions for Professional Chart Markup
1.  **Identify the Visual Turn:** Find the specific point where price drastically changed direction. 
2.  **Mark the Body Closes:** We mark the bodies, not just the wicks. Body closes are **"Closed Deals"**—the price where the market actually agreed to do business. Wicks are just **"Failed Attempts"**—the market went there, got rejected, and couldn't hold the line.
3.  **Trace the Push:** Look back to see where that specific momentum originated. If you see a "Drake candle" (a massive, aggressive bullish push), find the structural floor that supported it.
4.  **The Testing Tip:** Mark up your swing points manually first. Once you think you’ve got it, turn on the **Pivot Points High/Low** indicator. If the indicator matches your marks, you’re learning. If it doesn’t, go back and look at what you missed.

### The No Trade Zone and Incentive
The "No Trade Zone" is the range between your established Swing High and Swing Low. If price is bouncing around in the middle, **do not click a button.**
*   **Incentive:** Price stays in the No Trade Zone because there is "no new incentive." The market is always chasing a high or a low. Until price leaves that zone, it has no target, and you have no trade.

## 3. The Three-Step ICC Process: Indication, Correction, and Continuation

The ICC strategy is a logical blueprint. It’s not a "guess," it’s a sequence of events.

| Phase Name | Action/Definition | Rule for the Trader |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Indication** | A breakout where price punches through a swing level (High or Low). | **Do Not Trade.** This is just the "blueprint." It’s price showing you it has the strength to move. |
| **Correction** | A pullback after the indication. This is the market "shaking the Coke bottle." | **Wait and Monitor.** The market is collecting the "money for the move" (liquidity). |
| **Continuation** | Price resumes the trend and breaks the structural level again. | **Execute.** The "deals are closed," the liquidity is grabbed, and the path is clear. |

### The Logic of the Correction
Breaking a major level costs the market a lot of money. The "Correction" phase is the market's way of shaking the Coke bottle to build up pressure. It’s designed to punish **FOMO** and **Greed** traders. When amateurs see an Indication, they jump in without confirmation. The market then pulls back to wipe out their stop losses, collecting their money to fuel the real move (Continuation).

## 4. The Hierarchy of Timeframes: The 3-Timeframe Secret

The **4-Hour Chart** is the Ultimate Authority. It is the vehicle driving the trend. A "reversal" on the 15-minute chart is often just a healthy pullback on the 4-hour.

### The Leading Signal Insight
While the 4-hour is the authority, the lower timeframes (1-hour and 15-minute) are **leading indicators**. Once a 4-hour candle has finished its correction, you will see the 1-hour and 15-minute charts start to build new structure in the trade's direction *before* the 4-hour candle even closes. We use the lower timeframes to see the move building before the "Slow Nancies" even realize what’s happening.

### The Non-Negotiable Execution Process
1.  **Step 1 (4-Hour):** Determine the macro strength. Is the big vehicle moving up or down?
2.  **Step 2 (1-Hour):** Wait for the 1-hour structure to align. It must start making higher highs/lows (for a buy) in sync with the 4-hour.
3.  **Step 3 (15-Minute):** Verify perfect alignment. If the 4-hour is bullish, the 15-minute must be building bullish structure. 

**Rule:** If timeframes are fighting, you sit on your hands. Total alignment or no trade.
