## Part 1: Core Concepts and Principles

### The Foundation of Market Structure
Market structure is the "bread and butter" of trading. It is defined by the direction in which price moves and the patterns it forms. There are only two primary directions price can move to establish a trend:
1.  **Uptrend:** Characterized by price making **Higher Highs (HH)** and **Higher Lows (HL)**.
2.  **Downtrend:** Characterized by price making **Lower Highs (LH)** and **Lower Lows (LL)**.

### The Higher Timeframe Rule
The "cheat code" for successful trading is relying on higher timeframes (HTF). Structure on higher timeframes does not break quickly and is significantly more reliable than structure on lower timeframes.
*   **Primary Higher Timeframes:** 1-Hour (1H), 4-Hour (4H), and Daily.
*   **Rule of Strength:** The higher the timeframe, the stronger the structural rule becomes. Small timeframes frequently fail to respect these rules.

### Entry and Exit Rules
To maintain consistency and avoid "faking out," traders must follow specific entry prohibitions:
*   **Do Not Buy at a Higher High:** After a higher high is made, the market is expected to seek a higher low.
*   **Do Not Sell at a Lower Low:** After a lower low is made, the market is expected to seek a lower high.
*   **Entry Points:** Buy trades should ideally come from **Higher Lows**; sell trades should come from **Lower Highs**.

### The ICC Method
The ICC method is a repetitive cycle that defines how trends move and how trades are structured:

| Phase | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Indication (I)** | A new high or low is created, signaling a potential change or continuation in direction. |
| **Correction (C)** | A pullback where price creates a higher low (in an uptrend) or a lower high (in a downtrend). |
| **Continuation (C)** | Price resumes its original direction, breaking past the Indication level to form a new HH or LL. |

## Part 2: Technical Mechanics

### Timeframe Correlation ("The Team")
Price does not move "solo." For a trend to be high-probability, multiple timeframes must align or "match." 
*   **Team Concept:** If the 4H timeframe is bullish, the 1H and 15-minute timeframes should eventually align to show bullish sentiment before an entry is taken.
*   **The 15-Minute Detail:** The 15-minute timeframe is used to find the "small details" and monitor when a higher timeframe correction is ending.

### Support, Resistance, and the "Original Creator"
*   **Resistance (Sellers):** The level where sellers are positioned to push the price down. Buyers must "swap" orders with sellers to pass through.
*   **Support (Buyers):** The level where buyers are positioned to keep the market afloat.
*   **Original Creator:** This is the specific low that created a new high (or the high that created a new low). In a healthy trend, price should not break the level of the "Original Creator." If it does, the trend is considered reversed or invalid.

### Stop-Loss Placement
Stop-losses should be placed at levels where the trade’s logic becomes invalid:
*   **Short (Sell) Positions:** Place the stop-loss above the previous Lower High (the "creator" of the move).
*   **Long (Buy) Positions:** Place the stop-loss below the previous Higher Low.

## Part 3: Short-Answer Practice Questions

1.  **What are the two specific components required to identify an uptrend?**
    *   *Answer:* Price must be making Higher Highs (HH) and Higher Lows (HL).
2.  **Why is it considered a mistake to sell at a Lower Low (LL)?**
    *   *Answer:* Because after a LL is made, the market typically begins a correction to create a Lower High (LH), which could trap a seller at the bottom.
3.  **In the ICC method, what is the Indication level used for?**
    *   *Answer:* It serves as the signal of where price is trying to go and often acts as the entry level once price returns to it after a correction.
4.  **Which three timeframes are recommended for a "swing trader" to use as their primary combo?**
    *   *Answer:* The Daily, 4-Hour, and 1-Hour timeframes.
5.  **What must occur for a seller to successfully "bypass" a swing low?**
    *   *Answer:* Sellers must have more volume/pressure than the buyers sitting at that support level to break through it.
6.  **When monitoring a correction on a 15-minute chart, what signal indicates the correction is ending?**
    *   *Answer:* A change in direction where the 15-minute structure aligns back with the higher timeframe trend (e.g., making a new HH in a 4H uptrend).
7.  **What is the definition of a "Swing Low" in the context of the Daily timeframe?**
    *   *Answer:* A point where buyers stepped into the market with enough momentum to push the price back up, creating a visible "V" or pivot point.

## Part 5: Glossary of Important Terms

*   **Correction:** A temporary move against the main trend (a pullback) where the market "grabs more money" or orders before continuing.
*   **HH / HL:** Higher High and Higher Low; the building blocks of an uptrend.
*   **ICC:** Indication, Correction, Continuation; the primary three-step sequence for identifying and entering trades.
*   **Invalidation:** The point at which a trade setup is no longer logically sound, usually marked by price breaking a previous structural high or low.
*   **LH / LL:** Lower High and Lower Low; the building blocks of a downtrend.
*   **Market Structure:** The overall framework of highs and lows that dictates the trend and "rules" of price movement.
*   **Original Creator:** The specific pivot point (high or low) that caused the most recent structural breakout.
*   **Resistance:** An area on a chart where sellers are concentrated, often at previous highs.
*   **Support:** An area on a chart where buyers are concentrated, often at previous lows.
*   **Swing High/Low:** Significant peaks or troughs in price action used to identify major structural levels on higher timeframes.
*   **Timeframe Correlation:** The process of ensuring that different timeframes (e.g., 4H and 1H) are showing the same structural sentiment before taking a trade.
